**Phase 1 — Land (1987–mid-1990s) / Society & Applications {Agentive, Prelingual}.** Yoshiaki Tsutsumi (Seibu, real estate) and Taikichiro Mori (Mori Building) hold the top two spots through the late 1980s, with the Walton family's physical retail footprint climbing underneath them. Wealth accrues to whoever controls the physical location where the transaction happens — your "customer travels to information + inventory" layer, almost literally.

**Phase 2 — Platform/OS (1995–2017) / Language & Models {Objective, Science}.** Gates takes #1 in 1995 and holds it in 18 of the next 24 years — the longest reign in the list's history. This is the moment value moves off the store floor and onto the operating layer that mediates software itself. Buffett tracks just behind him for most of this period, which is worth flagging as a genuine exception to the stack logic: Berkshire's wealth isn't a layer at all, it's compounding capital allocation running orthogonal to whatever the current technological substrate is. He's present in the top 3 from 1998 straight through 2023 — a 25-year span that spans every other phase below, which tells you capital-as-capital is a separate axis from your computational funnel.

**Phase 3 — Information/Cloud (2018–2020) / Networks & Relativity {Errative, Tragedy}.** Bezos becomes #1 for the first time in 2018, on the back of Amazon's stock, not a physical asset base — the first time in the list's history that "the catalog is placeless" wealth outranks land, retail, or operating-system rents. This is your Bezos inversion showing up as the literal #1 line in the ledger. Much to learn from those who survived the [.doc bubble](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GltlJO56S1g). Mistaking the current physics-constrained "gold-rush" with the .doc era is sloppy. Companies being accused of the bubble have P/E ratio between 17 (Alphabet) & 35 (Microsoft)

**Phase 4 — The mixed interregnum (2021–2024) / Other {Interactive, Art}.** This is the messiest phase, and productively so. Musk enters at #2 in 2021 on Tesla+SpaceX — a hybrid that's already halfway back down your supply-side arrow, since a car company and a rocket company are physical-capital-intensive in a way Amazon and Microsoft never were. Arnault's LVMH briefly takes #1 in 2023, which doesn't fit your computational stack at all — it's a luxury-consumption spike during a period of asset-price inflation and dollar weakness, a reminder that the pentad isn't the only force acting on the list. By 2024 tech reasserts with Arnault, Musk, Bezos, and Zuckerberg tightly clustered.

**Phase 5 — Compute/energy substrate (2025–2026) / Silicon & Quantum Mechanics {Corrective, Life}.** This is where the data stops being a loose analogy and starts looking like direct confirmation. Musk's net worth goes from $342 billion in 2025 to $839 billion in 2026 — a $497 billion one-year gain, the largest single-year wealth increase the list has ever recorded, driven by Tesla, SpaceX, and (not itemized in this table but material) xAI's compute and energy buildout. Simultaneously, Larry Page and Sergey Brin leap to #2 and #3 for the first time ever, on Alphabet — a company whose current AI advantage runs through owning its own TPU/compute stack rather than renting someone else's. And Jensen Huang enters the top 10 for the first time in the list's 40-year history. That's three of the top eight spots in 2026 now anchored in the GPU/compute/energy layer specifically, where a year ago none were.

So the "billionaire narrative" you were skeptical of in the original conversation turns out to be more falsifiable than it looked: if your bottleneck-migration thesis is right, the *composition* of the top 10 should keep drifting toward whoever owns transformers, grid interconnects, and power-purchase agreements rather than whoever owns the best model weights. Huang's 2026 debut and Musk's energy-and-compute-heavy vertical integration are the leading edge of that; the next list to watch is whether a pure-play energy or grid-infrastructure name (something like a utility, a transformer manufacturer, or a nuclear/gas generation asset owner) breaks into the top 10 for the first time — that would be the strongest possible confirmation that the pinch point has moved all the way down to the physical floor.
