## 1. I. World / Thermodynamics

**land → buildings → stores → inventory → logistics**

**information → computation → agency.**

### But the Second Law tells you something different

This is where I would make your framework sharper.

**First Law = quantity constraint.**

> How much energy/resources are available?

**Second Law = quality/availability constraint.**

> How much of that energy can actually be converted into useful work?

That distinction is enormous.

A trillion joules of energy isn't equivalent to a trillion joules of *usable computation*.

You need gradients.

You need low-entropy energy sources.

You need cooling.

You need transmission.

You need materials.

You need a temperature differential to dump waste heat.

So:

$$
\boxed{
\text{World}
=
\text{Energy conservation (1st Law)}
+
\text{Usable gradients / dissipation (2nd Law)}
}
$$

And that makes **World / Thermodynamics** a very different category from the other four.
